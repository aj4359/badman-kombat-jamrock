# BADMAN KOMBAT — VISUAL NORTH STAR

Status: LOCKED for the next-gen vertical slice

## Thesis
BADMAN KOMBAT is a cinematic action game born from 1980s Kingston: heavyweight physical presence, human history, rain, concrete, sweat, sound-system pressure, tailored street style, martial discipline and a restrained cyber-future intrusion.

The game must never look like flat stickers, paper dolls, generic superheroes or a browser mock-up pretending to be combat.

## Visual pillars
1. HUMAN WEIGHT — fighters have believable mass, feet planted in the world, heavy hands, asymmetrical faces, scars, pores, sweat and fabric that reacts to motion.
2. KINGSTON MEMORY — architecture, signage, dancehall/sound-system culture, street tailoring, concrete, zinc, wood, tropical rain, sodium-vapour warmth and lived-in surfaces establish place before futuristic elements appear.
3. CONTROLLED FUTURE — cyan/teal technology is an intrusion and accent, not the whole palette. 1986 remains legible underneath it.
4. CINEMATIC DARKNESS — black levels, negative space, haze, practical lights, wet reflections and selective highlights create mystery without hiding gameplay readability.
5. CHARACTER BEFORE COSTUME — silhouette, posture, gait, expression and fighting rhythm identify a fighter before wardrobe or HUD does.
6. HISTORY IN EVERY BATTLE — entrances, scars, dialogue, arena details and crowd reactions imply that each fighter arrived with a life before the match.

## Colour and lighting grammar
Primary world: near-black, charcoal, wet concrete, aged timber, deep skin tones.
Warm practicals: amber/orange/sodium light, fire, tungsten interiors.
Future accent: restrained cyan/teal edge light and signage.
Hero contrast: warm-vs-cool split lighting on fighters.
Avoid rainbow neon, oversaturated cyberpunk and uniformly bright arenas.

## Camera grammar
Entrance: shoulder-height or low-angle tracking, 35–50mm feeling, deliberate movement, opponent revealed late.
Combat: readable 2.5D/3D composition with enough depth for circling, never a distant surveillance camera.
Impact: short impulse, hit-stop and selective close inserts; do not shake continuously.
Victory/KO: hold long enough to feel consequence.

## Leroy benchmark
Leroy is the first production-quality gate. Powerful Jamaican heavyweight/athletic frame; broad shoulders and traps; heavy forearms/hands; believable anatomy rather than superhero inflation. Mature, distinctive, asymmetrical face. Dark skin with physically believable roughness and sweat response. Period-authentic street tailoring adapted for combat. Gold only as a restrained personal detail. His idle stance must communicate experience before he attacks.

## Arena 01 — The Pit
Underground/repurposed Kingston venue. Circular or intimate fighting floor, timber/concrete barriers, multi-level spectators, haze, sweat, dust and rain carried in from outside. Crowd is close enough to feel dangerous. Warm overhead practicals with a cool rim source. Sound-system bass is part of the arena identity.

## Public-facing narrative
Core lines from the existing campaign remain useful direction:
- A cinematic action game inspired by 1980s Kingston.
- Real fighters. Real stories.
- Each fighter carries history. Each battle carries memory.
- Legacy isn't given — it's fought for.

"Always Bet on Black" may be used as campaign/brand language, but the game itself must earn that confidence through quality rather than relying on slogans.

## Quality gate
A hero fighter cannot be approved if:
- the body reads as primitive geometric construction at normal gameplay distance;
- skin/clothing lacks material separation;
- feet slide or float;
- the face is generic or symmetrical to the point of mannequin appearance;
- hands are visibly malformed;
- clothing/hair clips badly during core animations;
- silhouette is not identifiable in black;
- lighting makes dark skin unreadable;
- a still frame cannot stand beside premium fighting/action-game promotional imagery without looking like a placeholder.

## Hard prohibitions
No sprite/billboard fighters in the hero experience.
No CSS solution to a character-art problem.
No copying identifiable film/game characters or celebrity likenesses.
No generic 'cyberpunk city' replacing Kingston specificity.
No scaling the roster until Leroy + one opponent + The Pit pass the quality gate.
