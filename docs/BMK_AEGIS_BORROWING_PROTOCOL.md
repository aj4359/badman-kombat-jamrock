# BMK × AEGIS — Borrowing Protocol

BMK may borrow human-centred principles from AEGIS without importing AEGIS relationship-product mechanics or exposing proprietary implementation details.

## 1. First ask who's playing
AEGIS begins with the person before the search. BMK equivalent: before personalisation, invite the player to define a tiny optional Player Echo profile. The game must work fully without it.

## 2. Mirror: stated self vs demonstrated self
Never tell the player a psychological diagnosis. Compare only harmless stated preferences with observable game behaviour.
Example: player describes themselves as patient, but repeatedly rushes into fights. A trainer may tease the contradiction after sufficient evidence.

## 3. Timing matters
A remembered detail is not automatically used. Dialogue must consider relationship, scene, emotional state, recency and dramatic timing. The best personalisation can remain unused for hours.

## 4. Same underlying need, different expression
NPCs should respond to behavioural signals through their own personality. Two witnesses seeing the same act need not interpret it the same way. One sees courage; another sees recklessness.

## 5. Masks and performance
Characters may distinguish between how the player presents themselves and what repeated choices demonstrate, but BMK never claims access to the player's real personality. It reflects gameplay, not identity.

## 6. Uncertainty is preserved
The system should use language such as 'you fight like...', 'I've noticed...', or 'every time you're here...' rather than declaring psychological truths.

## 7. Consent and dignity
Allowed volunteered fields: nickname/preferred name, broad home region/city, non-sensitive tastes such as music affinity, optional play-style self-description.
Prohibited by design: financial data, passwords, contacts, private messages, microphone surveillance, precise location history, health data, religion, sexuality, political beliefs, ethnicity inference, biometric inference, or hidden scraping.

Player controls:
- personalisation off by default until explained/accepted
- each volunteered field optional
- inspect what BMK remembers
- delete individual volunteered details
- reset all Player Echo data
- core game remains available without personalisation

## 8. The surprise rule
Surprise comes from timing and continuity, never from secret data acquisition.
Bad: NPC reveals information the player did not knowingly provide or generate through gameplay.
Good: player voluntarily supplied a nickname two hours earlier; a recurring witness naturally uses it during a high-emotion scene.

## 9. Fourth-wall rule
Rare, authored, opt-in personalisation only. Never use traumatic, sensitive or inferred real-world information. A fourth-wall beat should feel uncanny because of craft, not surveillance.

## 10. Public promise
BMK should be able to say truthfully: **The world remembers how you played. It only knows about you what you chose to share.**
