# BADMAN KOMBAT — GAMEPLAY BECOMES EXPERIENCE

BMK is not designed as a sequence of mechanics with cinematic decoration. Mechanics are causes inside a living dramatic system.

## Experience loop
**PLAY → FEEL → WITNESS → REMEMBER → CONSEQUENCE → RECOGNITION**

A conventional combat event may end when damage is applied. A BMK event can continue through the room, witnesses and future encounters.

## Example: one heavy counter
1. Player reads Marcus and commits to the counter.
2. CombatFeel creates anticipation, active impact and recovery.
3. Camera/ImpactFX/audio/haptics communicate mass without obscuring readability.
4. CrowdDirector changes pressure.
5. Named witnesses interpret the event according to allegiance and temperament.
6. Relevant witnesses receive a memory tag.
7. The room settles; rain/breath/feet can become audible again.
8. Later dialogue/reputation/positioning may reference the witnessed event.
9. Player Echo may combine a safe volunteered detail with observed play style only when dramatic timing permits.

The punch therefore has a physical result, emotional result, social result and possible historical result.

## Experience Director
`src/game3d/ExperienceDirector.ts` is the orchestration layer between raw combat state and presentation/narrative systems. It does not own personal data and does not generate dialogue. It emits dramatic signals such as arrival, recognition, pressure, danger, consequence and aftermath.

This separation matters: combat remains deterministic/readable; personalisation remains bounded; presentation can become cinematic without changing damage rules.

## The fourth wall
BMK normally respects it. Rare recognition moments may bend it. Never constantly break it. The player's uncertainty is part of the effect.

## Budget philosophy
We cannot buy density at AAA scale. We can concentrate authored intelligence into fewer moments. One encounter with consequence is more valuable to the proof campaign than ten disposable encounters.

## Test
After a session, do not ask only: 'Was the combat fun?'
Also ask:
- Did The Pit feel like a place rather than an arena asset?
- Did anyone in the crowd feel like a person?
- Did an action appear to matter beyond health bars?
- Did the room ever seem to notice you?
- Is there a moment you want to tell somebody else about?

If the answer to the last question is yes, gameplay has started becoming experience.
